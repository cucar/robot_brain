This is Dan Abramov's "My Decade in Review" (Jan 2020), a personal retrospective covering 2010-2019.
Early career (2010-2012): Started college in Russia at 17, but dropped out after his first year when he landed a $18k/year job at a Russian-American outsourcing firm. After a burnout-induced quit and a chaotic camping trip in Crimea, he realized his desktop development skills were obsolete. He moved back in with his mom and joined an informal "bootcamp" (working on a mentor's projects for free in exchange for learning web development). A botched interview at his dream Russian social media company sent him into self-doubt — until Roman Mazurenko recruited him to work on Stampsy, a DIY publishing startup, in Moscow.
Stampsy years (2013-2015): Worked grueling hours building an iPad app that flopped despite being featured by Apple, then pivoted to a webapp. This is where he discovered React, started contributing to its ecosystem, gave his first (poorly-received) talks, and built a hot-reloading proof of concept that got traction on Twitter. In 2015, he created Redux specifically so he'd have something to demo at his first conference talk in Paris — which became a career-defining moment. He met Jing Chen there, got recruited to Facebook's London office, and married his girlfriend in a $100 wedding. Tragically, Roman Mazurenko was killed by a driver in Moscow the day after Dan arrived in London.
Facebook years (2016-2019): Brutal adjustment to a new country, language, and bureaucracy — including a panic attack during a US onboarding trip. Joined the React Core team as its only UK member, co-created Create React App, and spent 2017 helping rewrite React from scratch (React 16). Got hit with a surprise $30k tax bill on his Egghead course royalties. Got promoted, started his blog, and realized that explaining things matters to him as much as building them.
The piece is candid about privilege (upper-middle-class background, fitting the "coder stereotype"), explicitly avoids prescribing his path as advice, and doesn't glorify the unhealthy work patterns he describes.
